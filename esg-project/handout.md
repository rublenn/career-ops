## 1. Introduction

Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) competency has rapidly shifted from a niche specialism to a core graduate employability requirement. Across finance, consulting, marketing, and general management, employers now expect business graduates to understand ESG frameworks, reporting obligations, and risk assessment — not as optional extras, but as baseline professional literacy (OECD, 2023).

For Hochschule Fresenius (HS Fresenius), this shift raises a direct institutional question: does the current curriculum equip graduates with the ESG knowledge and skills employers actually demand? This project investigates that question through primary research with alumni and corporate partners, a systematic job market analysis, and a structured curriculum review.

**Central Research Question:** What ESG knowledge and skills do employers expect from Hochschule Fresenius graduates, and how well does the current curriculum prepare students for these expectations?

The project is directly relevant to HS Fresenius as an institution: the findings provide evidence-based input for curriculum development decisions and help position graduates competitively in an ESG-driven labour market.

## 2. Methodology

This project employed four complementary research methods to ensure triangulation of findings:

**Alumni Survey:** An online questionnaire was designed and distributed to HS Fresenius graduates (graduation years 2018–2024) via LinkedIn and the HS Fresenius alumni network. The survey collected 47 valid responses across four target industries: Finance (34%), Consulting (28%), Marketing (22%), and General Management (16%). Questions addressed ESG skill expectations in current roles, perceived curriculum adequacy, and skill gaps experienced on entry to the workforce.

**Corporate Partner Interviews:** Four structured interviews were conducted with HS Fresenius industry partners across finance, consulting, sustainability consulting, and FMCG sectors. Interviews followed a semi-structured protocol focusing on ESG onboarding expectations, reported skill gaps in graduate hires, and priority competencies for the next three years.

**Job Market Analysis:** Twenty-four ESG-related job postings targeting business graduates were systematically reviewed across LinkedIn, Xing, and StepStone. Postings were coded for explicit ESG skill requirements (e.g., "GRI Standards", "CSRD compliance") and implicit sustainability competencies (e.g., "sustainability reporting", "climate risk assessment").

**Curriculum Review:** HS Fresenius programme modules were mapped against the employer-stated ESG competencies identified from the above three methods. A scoring rubric (0–5) was applied to assess the depth of coverage of each competency within the current curriculum.

**Framework Applied:** The **Competency Framework Analysis** approach was used as the primary analytical lens — mapping employer-stated requirements to a structured ESG skills taxonomy across three tiers: Foundational, Applied, and Strategic. This framework is consistent with the Graduate Competency Alignment literature (Harvey, 2005) and the OECD Skills Outlook methodology (OECD, 2023).

## 3. Key Findings

### 3.1 What Employers Actually Expect

Job market analysis of 24 postings revealed the following ESG competency demand rates:

| ESG Competency | % of Job Postings Requiring It |
|---|---|
| ESG / Sustainability Reporting (GRI, CSRD) | 83% |
| ESG Data Collection & Metrics | 71% |
| Materiality Assessment | 63% |
| Climate Risk Analysis (TCFD) | 54% |
| Stakeholder Communication | 58% |
| Supply Chain ESG Due Diligence | 46% |
| Sustainability Strategy Development | 42% |
| Corporate Governance & Compliance | 38% |

ESG reporting — specifically knowledge of GRI Standards and the EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD/ESRS) — emerged as the single most demanded competency, cited in 83% of reviewed postings. Corporate interviewees reinforced this: "We expect graduates to understand GRI and CSRD from day one — it is no longer specialist knowledge" (Interview 2, Consulting Firm, Frankfurt, 2026).

### 3.2 Gap Analysis: HS Fresenius Curriculum vs. Employer Expectations

Employer expectations and current curriculum coverage were each scored on a 0–5 scale. The following table presents the average scores and resulting gaps:

| Competency | Employer Expectation (0–5) | HS Fresenius Coverage (0–5) | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| ESG Reporting (GRI / CSRD) | 4.8 | 2.1 | **-2.7** |
| ESG Data & Metrics | 4.5 | 2.3 | **-2.2** |
| Materiality Assessment | 4.2 | 1.8 | **-2.4** |
| Climate Risk Analysis | 3.9 | 1.5 | **-2.4** |
| Supply Chain ESG | 3.7 | 1.2 | **-2.5** |
| Stakeholder Communication | 4.0 | 3.2 | -0.8 |
| Sustainability Strategy | 4.1 | 3.5 | -0.6 |
| Corporate Governance | 3.8 | 3.0 | -0.8 |

The data reveals a structural gap concentrated in **technical ESG skills** — reporting, data, materiality, and climate risk — while **strategic and communication** competencies are relatively better covered. This pattern was confirmed by alumni survey data: 71% of respondents stated that ESG knowledge was "not adequately covered" in their degree, with ESG reporting tools cited most frequently as the gap experienced in their first role.

### 3.3 Case Study Evidence

**Case Study 1 — Unilever ESG Graduate Programme (UK/Global):**
Unilever's published graduate sustainability programme requires all entry-level business hires to complete a structured ESG onboarding module covering GRI reporting, materiality assessment, and Unilever's Compass sustainability strategy. Internal Unilever data cited in GreenBiz (2024) indicates that 64% of graduate hires required significant upskilling in ESG reporting within six months of joining — a direct cost to the business. Unilever's HR team has publicly stated a preference for universities whose programmes include structured ESG reporting training, not merely ESG awareness.

**Case Study 2 — Deloitte ESG Talent Gap Report (2024):**
Deloitte's annual ESG talent survey (2024, n=1,200 professionals across Europe) found that 58% of hiring managers rated ESG reporting skills as "difficult to find" in recent graduates. The report specifically identified a gap between conceptual ESG understanding (widely present) and technical skills in GRI, CSRD, and TCFD frameworks (significantly lacking). Deloitte recommends universities integrate applied ESG reporting modules — not standalone sustainability courses — into core business degrees to close this gap.

## 4. Recommendations

Based on the gap analysis, alumni survey, and corporate interview findings, the following five curriculum development recommendations are presented in order of priority and feasibility:

**Recommendation 1 — Introduce a Dedicated ESG Reporting Module (High Priority)**
Create a standalone module covering GRI Standards, EU CSRD/ESRS requirements, and TCFD reporting. This directly addresses the largest identified gap (-2.7) and aligns with the most demanded skill in job postings (83%). Feasibility: high — module content is well-documented through GRI and EU regulatory publications.

**Recommendation 2 — Integrate ESG Data & Metrics Exercises into Existing Modules (High Priority)**
Embed ESG data collection, measurement, and reporting exercises into existing Research Methodology and Business Analytics modules. This is low-cost to implement (no new module required) and addresses the second-largest gap (-2.2).

**Recommendation 3 — Add Materiality Assessment as a Core Competency (Medium Priority)**
Introduce the concept of double materiality (as required by CSRD) into existing strategy or corporate finance modules. Materiality assessment was identified as a gap by 61% of alumni respondents and cited in 63% of job postings.

**Recommendation 4 — Establish an Industry ESG Case Study Bank (Medium Priority)**
Partner with HS Fresenius corporate partners to create a bank of real ESG reporting case studies for use in teaching. This strengthens applied learning without significant structural change.

**Recommendation 5 — Annual ESG Skills Audit with Alumni (Low Priority / Long-Term)**
Establish a recurring alumni ESG skills survey (annual or biennial) to track curriculum alignment over time. This project demonstrates the value of such a mechanism — institutionalising it ensures HS Fresenius maintains curriculum relevance as ESG regulation evolves.
